## What is established (verify each)
- **Approval and start.** China approved the project in December 2024 (reported as 25 Dec 2024). Premier Li Qiang announced the start of construction at a groundbreaking in Nyingchi, Tibet, on **19 July 2025**, calling it a "project of the century".
  - Sources: Xinhua; ETV Bharat, 28 May 2026; The Week, 7 Aug 2026.
- **Scale.**
  - Five cascade hydropower stations on the lower Yarlung Tsangpo around the Great Bend, using a drop of about 2,000 m along a gorge of roughly 250 km.
  - Investment of about **1.2 trillion yuan (about US$167 billion)**. Other outlets give figures from $137bn to $170bn; use the official yuan figure and attribute it.
  - Output of about **300 billion kWh a year**, roughly three times the Three Gorges Dam. Capacity is widely reported as about 60 GW.
  - Run by a new central state-owned company, **China Yajiang Group** (Xinhua). Power is intended mainly for eastern China via ultra-high-voltage lines.
  - Completion is expected in the 2030s; one report says around 2033 (Daily Pioneer). Verify, or say no official date has been given.
- **Location and risk.**
  - Near the Great Bend in Medog (Motuo) County, Nyingchi. Reported as about 30 km from Arunachal Pradesh by the Daily Pioneer and "less than 50 km" by The Week.
  - The site is in a highly seismic zone at the Indian-Eurasian plate boundary; The Week names the Paizhen Fault.
  - A magnitude 7.1 earthquake near Shigatse (Dingri) on 7 January 2025 killed over 120 people. The magnitude is reported as 6.8 by China and 7.1 by the USGS, so attribute each.
  - The International Campaign for Tibet says China has not published environmental assessments.
- **China's position.**
  - The project is "run-of-the-river", "does not consume water", and will have "no negative impact on the lower reaches".
  - Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning: clean energy and climate goals.
  - Wang Lei, the chargé d'affaires in New Delhi, wrote in the Indian Express that China "does not and will never seek 'water hegemony'" and will release more data "in due course".
  - Find the primary Chinese Foreign Ministry transcript for these quotes.
- **India's position.**
  - The MEA (January 2025) urged China to ensure downstream states are not harmed and said India would monitor and protect its interests. Get the primary MEA statement.
  - Arunachal Chief Minister Pema Khandu has called it a potential "water bomb".
  - At the **35th WMCC (Beijing, 27 May 2026)** and **36th WMCC (6 Aug 2026)**, India pressed for an early meeting of the **Expert Level Mechanism (ELM) on trans-border rivers** (set up in 2006) and for sharing technical details of upstream projects.
  - Source: MEA press releases on the WMCC meetings, which are PRIMARY. Fetch both from mea.gov.in.
- **Data sharing.**
  - India-China hydrological data MoUs: the Brahmaputra MoU (2002; renewed 2008, 2013, 2018) **expired in 2023**, and the Sutlej MoU (2005) **expired in 2020**. Both are reported as not renewed because of the LAC standoff (Deccan Herald).
  - Under the MoU, China supplied flood-season data from 15 May to 15 Oct.
  - Data was halted once before, in 2017 after Doklam, and resumed on 15 May 2018.
  - December 2024 Special Representatives talks produced a six-point consensus that included cross-border rivers.
  - **UNRESOLVED: has the Brahmaputra MoU been renewed, or data sharing resumed, in 2025 or 2026?** Check MEA and Jal Shakti statements and the Lok Sabha/Rajya Sabha Q&A (sansad.in). The Rajya Sabha / Lok Sabha answers on "China's dam on Yarlung Tsangpo" are excellent primary sources.
- **Modi-Xi.** Xi visited New Delhi for the BRICS summit (12-13 Sept 2026), his first India visit in about seven years, and held bilateral talks with Modi. Reporting found does not mention rivers. **Check the MEA readout** for any mention of trans-border rivers.
- **India's counter: the Siang Upper Multipurpose Project (SUMP)**, according to ThePrint, 27 Oct 2026, by Moushumi Das Gupta:
  - About 11,200 MW (other reports say 11,000 MW), framed by the Jal Shakti ministry as storage and flood moderation.
  - Declared a national project in 2008; NITI Aayog proposed a single storage project in 2017.
  - NHPC's draft pre-feasibility report (30 Dec 2022) named three possible sites: Uggeng, Dite Dime and Parong. **The site is still not finalised.**
  - Adi and Galo communities oppose it. In May 2025, NHPC equipment was damaged and vehicles were burnt near Parong. A mob torched NHPC's Pasighat office (date to verify).
  - An NHPC official told ThePrint: "We have not moved an inch on the ground." A Rs 350 crore livelihood programme covers 26 villages.
  - The Congress opposes the project.
- **Wider context.**
  - Bangladesh has expressed concern; the Brahmaputra becomes the Jamuna there. See Asia News Network, Dhaka Courier and The Daily Star, and look for a Bangladesh foreign ministry statement.
  - Flash floods in Nepal's Bhote Koshi valley in August 2026 killed over 150 people (Daily Pioneer, 27 Aug 2026), renewing scrutiny of Tibetan dams. Verify the cause and the death toll from Nepal government or ICIMOD sources, and do not overstate any link to Medog.

## Key questions the piece should answer (with evidence, not assertion)
1. **How much of the Brahmaputra's water actually comes from Tibet?** Studies differ. The Tibetan share is a minority of the flow measured in Assam and Bangladesh, because of monsoon rain on the southern slopes, but it matters most in the lean season. Find peer-reviewed or Central Water Commission figures and attribute them. This is the crux of the "run-of-the-river, no harm" claim versus the "water bomb" fear.
2. **What can a run-of-the-river cascade still do?** Daily flow regulation (peaking), sediment trapping (silt that builds Assam's and Bangladesh's floodplains and the delta), dam safety in an earthquake zone, and the absence of data and early warning.
3. **Why has it stayed out of the spotlight?** It was announced years ago; construction is remote and opaque; the story has been crowded out by the India-China thaw; the harms are slow; and there is no water-sharing treaty or international forum, since neither country has joined the 1997 UN Watercourses Convention. Verify that last point.
4. **Scenarios** (conditional, with drivers):
   - (a) Managed coexistence: data MoUs are renewed and the ELM meets.
   - (b) Strategic leverage: data is withheld during crises and lean-season releases are used as pressure.
   - (c) Disaster risk: a seismic or landslide-dam event with no warning system.
   - (d) A dam race: SUMP goes ahead despite local opposition, with ecological costs and displacement of the Adi.
   Attribute forecasts to named analysts. Uttam Kumar Sinha (MP-IDSA) told ETV Bharat that China prefers revocable "arrangements, mechanisms, MoUs".
5. **What to watch** (dated where possible):
   - the next ELM meeting
   - renewal of the Brahmaputra data MoU before the 2027 flood season (15 May)
   - the next WMCC and Special Representatives talks
   - SUMP's pre-feasibility report and site decision
   - China's next five-year plan (2026-30) milestones for the project
   - Bangladesh's diplomacy
   - monsoon 2027 flood data
